Feasibility & design study

Getting audio off the device, and getting off Forge

Three options investigated: direct BLE from Linux, an iPhone app built without a Mac, and running the whole pipeline inside GitHub. Each is assessed against what the code, the vendor SDK and the vendor's own documentation actually say — not against what the plan hoped.

What changes the question

Five findings landed before any option could be assessed on its own terms. Three of them constrain both device options equally, one is bad news for the seamless requirement, and one is unexpectedly good news about cost.

1. The official SDK cannot remove Plaud's cloud Blocker for the stated goal

This is cryptographic, not contractual. Two calls in the compiled SDK interface always reach Plaud's servers:

  • signDeviceSn(deviceType:sn:) — the BLE bind handshake needs a server-issued signature over the device serial, which the firmware validates. Not forgeable. connectBleDevice(bleDevice:_ devToken:…) takes that token as an argument.
  • generateRsaKeyPair(completion:) returns PlaudPartnerGenKeyResponse{publicKey, privateKey} — the keypair that decrypts your recordings is minted server-side and downloaded.

I checked whether there was an escape hatch: PlaudBleSDK also exposes generateRSAKeyPair(sizeInBits:) and setKeys(publicKey:privateKey:), which looked like a local-keygen path. It isn't — those belong to a vendored SwiftyRSA-style primitives library (PrivateKey, ClearMessage, Padding, notAPrivateKey), not an alternative to the server-minted E2EE key.

What this means precisely: audio and transcription can be entirely self-hosted — decryption and Opus decoding are local, and Plaud's own transcription API is optional. Bind and key issuance cannot. "Omit the use of Plaud Cloud" is achievable for the data path and not for the trust path.

2. Both device options replace the working path rather than adding one Applies to 1 and 2

Plaud's docs are explicit: "Plaud devices can only be bound to one application at a time." Your own ADR-001 established the other half — iOS has no BLE promiscuous mode, so a custom app cannot observe the official app's sync; it must become the BLE peer.

So either option costs you the official app for that device: its sync, its firmware updates, its Wi-Fi provisioning UI — and, if it exists for this model, its background auto-transfer. This was the sharpest cost in ADR-001 and it hasn't changed.

3. Background BLE bulk transfer does not work the way the plan assumes Kills "seamless" on iOS

iOS gives a backgrounded BLE app roughly 10 seconds per wake, and Punch Through's measurements put best-case BLE throughput at about 50 kB/s. A 10 MB recording is therefore ~3.5 minutes at a perfect rate and ~17 minutes at a realistic one — stitched together from 10-second wakes.

The decisive evidence is that Plaud themselves route around this. Their "Fast Transfer" abandons BLE for Wi-Fi and the docs say "Keep the Plaud App open until the transfer completes." Their background "Auto-transfer" needs Always-location permission — i.e. it is region/beacon wake, not pure Core Bluetooth — and is documented as requiring Note Pro firmware 1.6+. The NotePin S is not named.

This constraint is iOS-side and applies whether you build on a Mac or on Linux. It is the single most likely reason option 2 fails.

4. ADR-001's own trigger conditions have already fired The ADR is stale

ADR-001 declined an iPhone app because "two independent transports already cover each other." Both are now gone. docs/transport-tests.md records T1 (charging, phone untouched) and T2 (unlocked, app not opened) as failures, with T3 (app foregrounded) succeeding immediately. The verdict there is unambiguous: "Sync requires the Plaud app in the foreground."

That is worse than trigger 2 anticipated. Whatever you decide, ADR-001 should be superseded rather than left standing as the project's answer.

5. Option 3's cost premise is wrong — in your favour Verified locally

You assumed moving to GitHub means losing subscription-based agent usage and paying per-token API prices. That isn't so. claude setup-token exists on this machine and its help text reads "Set up a long-lived authentication token (requires Claude subscription)", and the official anthropics/claude-code-action accepts a claude_code_oauth_token on Pro/Max.

Whether the agent costs $0/month or ~$230/month in Actions is therefore an authentication choice, not a consequence of the move. The one unverified risk is whether subscription limits tolerate ~150 CI agent runs a month — Anthropic publishes no CI-specific policy.

Option 1 — Direct BLE from Linux

Verdict: the most technically achievable of the three, and the one with a genuine terms-of-service contradiction at its centre.

You are further along than you may realise

Your own ble-protocol-notes.md and ble-file-transfer.md already map the protocol from Plaud's publicly-published Apache-2.0 Android SDK. The download path is known:

the transfer sequence, already reverse-engineered
service 0x1910   char 0x2BB0 (subscribe, device→us)   char 0x2BB1 (write, us→device)

req  1  HandShakeReq          → read portVersion from HandShakeRsp bytes 4–5
req 26  GetRecSessionsReq     → list recordings; sessionId=0 means "all"
req 28  SyncRecFileStartReq   → begin transfer at an offset
        protocolType-2 frames → the audio: bare Opus, 80 bytes × ch per 20 ms
req 29  SyncRecFileStopReq    → end / CRC
req 30  SyncRecFileDelReq     → delete from device

Audio arrives as bare Opus packets with no container — fixed 80 bytes per 20 ms, so the stream is self-delimiting and byte offsets are exactly seekable. You mux Ogg yourself or hand raw Opus to ffmpeg. Resumable transfer falls out of that for free, which matters for a proximity-triggered design where the device can walk out of range mid-file.

Throughput is not a problem here

Unlike iOS, Linux/BlueZ has no 10-second background budget. Opus at 16 kHz mono is 4 kB/s of audio, so even a conservative 20 kB/s of BLE throughput is ~5× realtime. A 40-minute recording (~9.6 MB) is roughly 8 minutes of transfer — long, but it runs unattended while you're in the room, and it resumes if interrupted.

Forge is the better host, not the laptop

I checked: Forge has a working Bluetooth controller. 3C:EF:A5:60:E8:D6 forge, not rf-killed, and a 25-second scan saw 35 BLE devices. For a proximity design this beats a laptop outright — Forge is always on, mains-powered, never sleeps, and is already the agentic host, so no new hop is introduced. The trade is that a desktop sits in one room; you have to walk past it.

Either way the architecture is unchanged: this is the ingest role, and the design already treats roles as capabilities rather than hostnames (./ops/install.sh ingest). WarDog is on the tailnet but refused SSH, so I could not confirm its BLE hardware.

The proximity trigger

Two viable shapes. A continuous passive scan with a detection callback (bleak supports this) fires the moment the pin advertises — lowest latency, keeps the radio in scan mode, which is irrelevant on a mains-powered desktop. Or a systemd timer that simply attempts a connect every few minutes and fails fast when out of range — cruder, but it reuses the timer/lock/alarm machinery the pipeline already has, and needs no new failure modes. I'd start with the timer.

Wi-Fi as the bulk transport — a real improvement, and a corrected claim

The SDK exposes a Wi-Fi path, and it changes option 1's shape for the better. The sequence is bleWiFiOpen(status, wifiName, wholeName, wifiPass) — provision network credentials to the device over BLE — and then a WiFiAgent with its own getFileList, syncFile, stopSyncFile and deleteFile.

So BLE becomes the control channel and Wi-Fi the data channel. That removes the throughput concern entirely for long recordings and, more usefully, removes the requirement to stay in BLE range for the whole transfer — the pin joins your network and the pull happens at LAN speed.

Correcting a claim from my own research. One pass reported an exportAudioViaWiFi API and recommended, as its top priority, investigating whether the pin could push audio to a host you control — which would have deleted this whole problem. I grepped the SDK interface: no such symbol exists. The Wi-Fi surface is WiFiAgent.syncFile, i.e. a client pulling from the device, not the device uploading to an arbitrary endpoint. You still need a client.

The device→cloud Wi-Fi upload that ADR-001 described does exist as a product feature, but your own T1 test disproved it for this pin — charging with the phone untouched produced no sync. So that door is already known to be closed.

The blockers, in order of severity

BlockerStatusNotes
The bind signatureHard Firmware validates a server-issued signature over the serial. You cannot forge it. You must obtain it from Plaud's portal — which is free and self-serve, but see the contradiction below.
ChaCha20-Poly1305Unknown If the pin reports portVersion ≥ 20, every frame is encrypted and the key exchange is undecoded. Your own notes flag this as unresolved and tied to the RSA question.
Unbound-device behaviourUntested Whether requests 26/28 work at all before a handshake is unanswerable without hardware in a pairing state. ingest/ble_scan.py exists to answer it.
portVersionManageable Selects frame layout, list stride, and whether ChaCha applies. Read it at runtime from HandShakeRsp; never hardcode. Your notes already give a runtime cross-check.
No prior artReal cost Nobody has publicly reverse-engineered this BLE protocol. Every community project scrapes Plaud's cloud after the official app has already uploaded — the opposite of what you want. You would be first.

The contradiction worth thinking hard about. Option 1 needs the bind signature, which needs a Plaud Embedded portal account. Those terms prohibit, verbatim, reverse engineering "the source code, firmware, or underlying protocols" and circumventing "device authentication, pairing, or activation mechanisms." But option 1 is a reimplementation of the protocol from a decompiled SDK.

So the two halves of option 1 pull against each other: accept the terms to get the credential, and the credential's terms forbid the client you'd use it with. There may be readings where interoperating with hardware you own is fine — the SDK is Apache-2.0 and vendor-published, and your notes make that argument. I'm not able to resolve it for you, and it's worth a careful read of the current terms before investing weeks. It's a risk to weigh, not a settled prohibition.

And there is a second clause that is broader than the circumvention one. The terms also prohibit using the SDK to develop "a general-purpose or vertical-specific AI note-taking, meeting capture, meeting summarization, or conversation intelligence application." Read plainly, that describes Atticus. It applies to option 2 as much as option 1, since both need a portal account, and the practical exposure is account termination rather than anything dramatic. Worth knowing before you build a dependency on it.

Effort: substantial. The framing, command table and transfer sequence are done, but you'd be implementing a binary protocol with four layout variants, an undecoded crypto layer, and no reference implementation to diff against. Budget weeks, not days, and expect the first milestone to be "can I get a session list at all."

Option 2 — iPhone without a Mac

The Mac question is answered, and it's the easy part. Mac-free iOS development is genuinely viable in 2026 for exactly this shape of app. That is not where this option fails.

The Mac-free toolchain that works

Arch Linux → signed IPA on a stock iPhone
Swift 6.3.3            AUR: swift-bin
xtool 1.17.0           MIT, builds via SwiftPM, signs with a bundled zsign fork
usbmuxd                USB transport
pymobiledevice3 10.3.0 install, logs, HCI traces
+ an Xcode .xip you download yourself   ← the licence problem lives here

Free-Apple-ID 7-day certificates work entirely over HTTP from Linux — no Mac, no Xcode GUI. Anisette runs natively (xtool extracts libCoreADI.so from Apple's own Android Apple Music APK). Installing a signed IPA needs no jailbreak: pymobiledevice3 apps install. Logging is genuinely good — idevicesyslog for unified logs, and pymobiledevice3 btlogger dumps Bluetooth HCI to pcapng, which is directly useful for this project.

What breaks: no Simulator ever (moot — it has no Core Bluetooth anyway); no actool, so asset catalogs need loose PNGs and a hand-written CFBundleIcons; no .xcodeproj (must be a Package.swift project); SwiftData @Model is a hard blocker; and an open xtool bug (#197) on Arch breaks @Observable, which would gut a SwiftUI app. Debugging is printf-plus-logs — no confirmed end-to-end Linux→iOS 26 lldb session has been published.

The licence, verbatim from the Xcode SLA: "USE OF APPLE SOFTWARE IS … AUTHORIZED ONLY FOR EXECUTION ON AN APPLE-BRANDED PRODUCT RUNNING MACOS." Every Linux path that consumes the Apple SDK breaches this. Your older Mac makes that go away.

The crux — background BLE

Good news first: iOS 26 improved this. Apple's TN3115 (revised 2025-09-15) shows force-quit apps can now be relaunched if they adopt AccessorySetupKit — an Apple engineer confirmed this is an addition, not a regression, and the European Commission's DMA factsheet confirms it is worldwide. AccessorySetupKit needs Info.plist keys only, no entitlement, so it works on a sideloaded build. Background modes are available to free personal teams too — UIBackgroundModes is a plist property, not an entitlement.

Then the wall. The 10-second wake budget and ~50 kB/s ceiling mean bulk audio over background BLE is tens of minutes of stitched wakes. Every confirmed alternative is a dead end: silent push is "heavily throttled and will not be guaranteed" per Apple DTS (and unavailable on free signing); BGProcessingTask requires idle+charging so can't be a proximity trigger; BGContinuedProcessingTask (new in iOS 26) "starts in the foreground"; BLE accessories aren't part of MFi at all.

And an unresolved risk that alone justifies the $99: every 7-day re-sign reinstalls the app, and whether that destroys pending Bluetooth state-restoration is undocumented — an Apple forum thread asking exactly this has zero replies. If it does, the app needs a manual launch to re-arm, which defeats "the user never opens it."

Before writing any code — a two-day spike

Three unknowns can each kill this, and all are cheap to test:

  1. Does the NotePin S support background auto-transfer at all? Enable it in the official app with Always-location granted. Plaud documents it for Note Pro firmware 1.6+ and doesn't name the NotePin S. If the toggle doesn't appear or doesn't fire, the seamless requirement is dead at the hardware level and no iOS engineering fixes it. Test this first.
  2. Does @Observable compile under xtool on Swift 6.3.3? (xtool #197.)
  3. Will xtool correctly embed and sign a plain dynamic .framework? Plaud ships four .framework bundles — not .xcframework, which is the only thing SwiftPM binaryTarget accepts, and xcodebuild -create-xcframework is macOS-only. This is the highest-risk unverified step in the Linux path.

Costs, and two things to check about the older Mac

The $99 Apple Developer Program is effectively mandatory, for three independent reasons: ~1-year signing instead of a 7-day cycle; the undocumented re-sign/restoration risk above; and the Hotspot Configuration entitlement, which is not available to a free personal team and which Plaud's Wi-Fi Fast Transfer needs. Without it a free-signed build is BLE-only, i.e. stuck behind the throughput wall.

Plaud Embedded pricing is ambiguous in a way that matters. The free tier covers 50 device connections and 300 transcription hours, after which it is $15 per connected device per month. Whether "connection" means a device registration (one, for you, likely $0 forever) or a BLE session (which you'd exhaust in a week of walk-past syncing) is not something I could pin down. Worth clarifying with them before designing around $0.

Check the older Mac before counting on it. Xcode 26.x requires macOS Tahoe 26, which drops every Intel Mac except the 2019 16" MacBook Pro, the 2020 13" (4×TB3), and 2020-or-later iMac/mini/Mac Pro. If yours can't run Tahoe it cannot build for an iOS 26.6 phone without DeviceSupport-file transplants. And note a Mac buys you a debugger, not BLE testing — the iOS Simulator has had no Core Bluetooth since iOS 7, so all BLE work is on-device either way.

A ~$500 Mac mini M4 beats every cloud Mac if the debug loop becomes intolerable, because a cloud Mac cannot see your BLE peripheral or your USB iPhone. Running total if you build this: $99/yr Apple plus possibly $15/device/month Plaud — call it $99–$279/yr before any hardware.

Option 3 — Removing Forge

You said you don't intend to do this, so this is scoped as "is it possible, and what would it cost." Short answer: the processing half ports cleanly. The publishing half and the ingest half do not — and the cost isn't the obstacle you expected.

Three blockers, two landing on the same plan gate

BlockerSeverityEscape
No private rendered HTML below GitHub Enterprise Cloud HardGHEC private Pages, or keep a private host
Ingest needs a stateful 146 MB browser profile HardDevice-direct ingest (option 1 or 2), or keep Forge
Approval gate (environment reviewers) is public-repo-only on Free/Pro/Team SoftGHEC, or a manual workflow_dispatch

Publishing is the one that actually stops you. GitHub's own words: "GitHub Pages sites are publicly available on the internet, even if the repository for the site is private." Private Pages requires GHEC plus an org-owned project site. The fallbacks don't work either — GitHub "don't directly support rendered views of commits to HTML documents", and raw.githubusercontent.com serves text/plain with nosniff and a sandbox CSP, so it renders as source forever. For reports derived from ambient microphone audio, a publicly readable URL is not a trade you should make.

The ingest coupling — confirmed

I suspected this and it checks out for five independent reasons. Runners are ephemeral VMs; secrets cap at 48 KB so the profile can't be one; actions/cache is the only mechanism and "you cannot change the contents of an existing cache" plus 7-day eviction directly contradicts a profile that mutates on login and refreshes over 30 days; GITHUB_TOKEN can't write secrets so a refreshed session can't be persisted; and committing the profile would put live Plaud session cookies in the same repo the agent can read — the exact inversion of the credential-free-agent design.

Therefore a GitHub-only pipeline requires a device-direct ingest. Options 1/2 and option 3 are not independent — option 3 depends on one of them.

The dominant Actions cost is a rule, not a rate: minutes are rounded up to the whole minute, per job, so a 5-second cron poll bills a full minute. A 5-minute cron would cost ~$42/month in overage; on: push, paths: ['inbox/**'] costs nothing within Pro's 3,000 minutes and reacts in seconds. Use push, never cron.

If you ever did build it

job split — the agent never holds a credential
on: push, paths: ['inbox/**']     # not cron: rounding makes polling expensive

job triage    permissions: {contents: write}
              transcribe → wake gate → gated? STOP, agent never runs
              passed? repository_dispatch  # GITHUB_TOKEN pushes fire nothing

job agent     permissions: {}            # deny everything
              NO id-token: write         # injects ambient OIDC vars any subprocess can mint
              checkout with persist-credentials: false   ← default is TRUE and hands the agent git push
              transcript passed as a FILE, never interpolated into ${{ }}
              docker run --network none --read-only --cap-drop=ALL
              emits a PROPOSAL as an artifact

job apply     permissions: {contents: write}
              validates the proposal against a schema, commits, publishes

Two security notes worth keeping even if you never migrate. actions/checkout defaults to persist-credentials: true — its own docs say "the auth token is persisted in the local git config" — so a checkout step alone would hand your agent a working git push. And id-token: write is a liability on an agent job, not a win: it injects ambient request variables that any subprocess can redeem for an arbitrary audience.

I verified both your repos already use the safe read default for GITHUB_TOKEN, not the legacy permissive setting that pre-Feb-2023 repos can still carry. Two genuine upsides also exist: each job is a fresh ephemeral VM destroyed afterwards, which is stronger containment than Forge's persistent host, and GitHub Agentic Workflows (preview, Claude is a supported engine) already implements this architecture — read-only by default, secrets isolated in downstream jobs, writes only via validated safe-outputs.

Honest framing: this is not a Forge-elimination project, it's a Forge-shrinking one. Because ingest can't port, Forge survives as an ingest-only host unless a device option replaces it — and that outcome is worse on your own ADR-003 axis, since the Plaud credential still lives on a host you maintain while the agent moves somewhere you can't inspect at 3 a.m. You'd also trade journalctl and a shell for 90-day log retention.

Recommendation

Do the cheap experiment before any of the expensive ones. One test discriminates between all three options, costs an evening, and needs no code:

Enable Auto-transfer in the official Plaud app with Always-location granted, and see whether the NotePin S syncs in the background. Plaud documents this feature for Note Pro firmware 1.6+ and does not name your model. If it works, you may not need any of these options — the transport problem you're solving would already be solved by a setting. If it doesn't appear or doesn't fire, that's a firmware-level answer that also tells you option 2's seamless requirement is unreachable.

If that fails, my ranking is:

  1. Option 1 (direct BLE on Forge) — highest chance of actually delivering what you described. No 10-second budget, no App Store, no re-signing, no Mac, and Forge is always on with working Bluetooth. Resumable transfer falls out of the Opus framing, which suits walk-past ingest. The costs are real: weeks of binary-protocol work, an undecoded crypto layer, no prior art, and the terms contradiction above deserves a careful read first.
  2. Option 2 (iPhone) — only if the spike shows the NotePin S does support background auto-transfer, because otherwise you're building against a throughput wall that Plaud's own engineers avoided. If you do proceed, pay the $99 and keep the older Mac for the debug loop; verify it runs macOS Tahoe 26 first, since Xcode 26 requires it.
  3. Option 3 (GitHub) — interesting to know, and I'd leave it. It can't publish privately below GHEC, it can't do ingest at all, and it costs you the observability that made today's six production bugs findable.

And regardless of which you pick: supersede ADR-001. Its rationale rests on two transports that testing has since disproved, and leaving it as the project's standing answer will mislead whoever reads it next — including you, in six months.

One thing none of these options fixes: whichever transport wins, ambient audio still lands permanently in git history. That was the audit's open privacy item and it's independent of this decision.

What was verified, and what wasn't

Verified directly on this machine

  • Forge's Bluetooth controller exists, is not blocked, and completed a 25-second scan seeing 35 devices. No device advertising service 0x1910 was visible — expected, since the pin is currently bound to the iPhone and so isn't advertising for new connections. This does not prove the pin is reachable; that needs it in a pairing state.
  • claude setup-token exists and requires a Claude subscription — the basis for option 3's cost-neutral path.
  • Both repos use default_workflow_permissions: read.
  • The vault is 10 MB after ~24 recordings (~0.45 MB each), extrapolating to ~270 MB/month at 20/day.
  • The Plaud SDK's cloud dependency, read from the compiled .swiftinterface: signDeviceSn, generateRsaKeyPair returning a private key, and connectBleDevice(…devToken…). I also checked and disproved a possible escape hatch — PlaudBleSDK.generateRSAKeyPair is a vendored RSA primitives library, not a local alternative to the server-minted E2EE key.
  • ADR-001's trigger conditions against docs/transport-tests.md.

Researched, not independently reproduced

  • iOS background-BLE behaviour, TN3115's iOS 26 relaunch table, sideloading status, and xtool's capabilities and open bugs.
  • GitHub Actions pricing, the per-job minute rounding, Pages visibility rules, and the cache constraints.
  • Plaud's Auto-transfer firmware requirement and the Fast Transfer foreground requirement.

Known unknowns that would change the answer

  • Whether the NotePin S reports portVersion ≥ 20 (ChaCha20 on every frame).
  • Whether an unbound pin honours requests 26/28 at all.
  • Whether Claude subscription limits tolerate ~150 CI agent runs/month.
  • Your GitHub org plan — not readable with the current token, and it decides both GHEC-gated blockers.
  • WarDog's Bluetooth hardware — the host refused SSH.
